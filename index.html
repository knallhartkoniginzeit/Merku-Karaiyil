A Coming of Age Drama

Merku Karaiyil

[LOG LINE]

Set in 2015 Trichy, Ram aka Perry (13), Hari aka Keeri (13), and David aka Dhadi (13) are three inseparable boys escaping broken homes and constant emotional pressure, united by one small dream to see the sea once in their lives. When they run away toward Varkala, fate brings them together with Chandru, a quiet, emotionally distant man who unexpectedly becomes their guide and protector. As the journey unfolds, Chandru witnesses their innocence, friendship, and unfiltered joy, while silently battling a deep guilt he cannot share. After helping the boys fulfill their dream, reunite with their families, and heal their wounds, Chandru walks away alone finally at peace. Only in the final moments do we realize the truth. Chandru is actually an older Perry, who has been living with unbearable guilt from his college days, where Keeri and Dhadi died in an accident. This miraculous journey with their younger selves allows him to say the apology he never could, receive forgiveness without revealing the truth, and finally let go turning a childhood road trip into a deeply emotional story about friendship, regret, and healing across time.

[SYNOPSIS]

2014, Trichy. Ram aka Perry (13), Hari aka Keeri (13), and David aka Dhadi (13) are inseparable friends. Life at home is difficult for all three. Perry lives with a strict mother and an often-absent father, Keeri grows up surrounded by constant fights, and Dhadi lives with his alcoholic father. Their homes are filled with tension, and the only place they feel safe and happy is with each other. Whenever Perry feels overwhelmed, he escapes to an old theatre to watch movies alone. In the same colony lives Varipuli (24), a timid man with ADHD and Tourette syndrome who is looked down upon by everyone, including his uncle, who runs a wholesale-load business using a Tata Ace. Though Varipuli does most of the hard work, he is never respected. The three boys are the only ones who treat him with kindness, and their friendship means everything to him. After the half-yearly holidays, a rich and arrogant classmate, Arjun (13), mocks the boys for never having seen a beach. He boasts about his family trip to Varkala, knowing they have never been there. Hurt and embarrassed, the boys promise themselves that one day they will visit a beach, take photos, and prove him wrong. Around the same time, Perry hears a rumour that actor Sivakarthikeyan will attend their school annual day. The news spreads throughout the school, but on the day, the students discover that it was false. Arjun humiliates the boys again, and the next day Perry loses his temper and hits him. Their parents are called, a heated argument breaks out between the families, and the boys are forbidden from meeting each other. Perry's mother even decides to change his school and move houses after the exams. Fearing separation and fed up with their homes, the boys decide to run away after their exams and go to Varkala. When Varipuli discovers their plan, he secretly agrees to help, believing it will only be a short trip. Meanwhile, Chandru (26), an IT professional, struggles with guilt from his past. One night, after receiving a voice note from Kavya (25), who secretly loves him, Chandru avoids her birthday party invitation and drives away in the rain. At the same time, Varipuli hides the boys in the back of his Tata Ace and drives toward Kerala. After a misunderstanding at the Madurai toll, the boys get stranded on the highway. Later that night, Chandru attends Kavya's birthday celebration. Seeing her surrounded by happy friends triggers painful memories, and when Kavya confesses her feelings, he walks away, believing he doesn't deserve happiness. Lost in the rain, the boys suddenly appear in front of Chandru's speeding car. A passing comet lights up the dark road just in time, allowing Chandru to stop inches away from them. The boys lie about their situation and convince him to take them to Varkala. Reluctantly, he agrees.
The journey begins with constant clashes between Chandru and Perry. Meanwhile, their parents report them missing to the police. During the journey, the boys help a girl injured in a road accident and take her to the hospital, where they meet Murugan (35+), a stranded man who asks them for a ride to Trivandrum. They agree, unaware that he is involved in statue smuggling. Back in Trichy, Varipuli speaks honestly to the boys' parents about the pressure their children have been living under. Slowly, the parents begin to realize their own mistakes. In Trivandrum, Murugan's actions lead the boys into trouble, and they are taken to a police station. There, they meet Varadhan (45), who recognizes them from their school and rescues them. He takes them to his adopted daughter's reception. At the reception, the boys learn about Varadhan's troubled past, which unknowingly mirrors Chandru's own guilt. Their presence brings Varadhan closer to his family. That night, Chandru gets drunk and opens up emotionally. The next morning, Chandru becomes furious when he discovers that the boys went to a nearby lake and slaps Perry. Hurt, the four silently leave Varadhan's house. Meanwhile, the parents learn that Varipuli knew about the boys' plan and decide to travel to Kerala. The police advise them to go to Kerala the next morning. That night, boys find a room to stay the night. Perry clashes with Chandru again and storms out, misunderstanding his friends and leaving the room. The boys search desperately. Perry ends up in an old theatre, where a drunk man tries to misbehave with him but others intervene. The boys finally find Perry at the theatre exit and reunite emotionally. Touched by their bond, Chandru listens as the boys admit the truth they just want to go home. Chandru agrees to help them. The next day, while their parents travel from Trichy, Chandru secretly takes the boys to Varkala instead. When they realize it, the boys are overwhelmed. They run into the sea, laughing and playing freely. Chandru watches, smiling through pain, haunted by memories of two friends who died years ago in an accident involving him. The boys hug him and tell him it was not his fault. It becomes a healing moment for all of them. As Chandru goes to get towels, the parents arrive at the beach. Expecting anger, the boys are met with hugs and apologies. Everyone forgives each other. Chandru watches from a distance and quietly leaves, his heart finally at peace. The boys return home with their parents. Chandru drives alone on the highway, tears in his eyes this time, happy ones. A comet passes across the sky once again, marking a new beginning.
A few days later, Chandru prepares for a date with Kavya. Before leaving his house, he looks at a photo frame and smiles. The camera slowly reveals the photo it is Perry, Keeri, and Dhadi in their school uniforms. As the camera moves around the room we see many photo frames on the wall of Perry with his parents and pictures of Perry, Keeri, and Dhadi growing up together from childhood to college. These visuals are intercut with Chandru’s voice over, reflecting on his journey and memories. Chandru is no stranger. He is Ram. His full name is Ramachandran.

[SOUNDTRACK]

The story blends fun, energetic, melancholic, and emotional sounds, creating a dynamic musical experience. It explores diverse styles from pop and ghazals to regional songs and original score to constantly engage the audience.

Director

Dilip

Dilip is an emerging writer, director, and actor from Trichy, currently based in Chennai. He’s driven by character-centred storytelling and a fascination with the emotional complexities of everyday life. With experience as an Assistant Director across film projects and as a director of independent short films, he is drawn to stories that find depth in ordinary people, relationships, and seemingly simple moments. His cinematic sensibility embraces natural landscapes, understated emotions, and the delicate balance between humour and melancholy. ‘Merku Karaiyil’ marks his most ambitious step yet, bringing together the innocence of childhood, the bond of friendship, the complexities of family, the weight of guilt, and the quiet longing of waiting for love, all unfolding against the beauty of the South Indian landscape.
